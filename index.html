How Physicists Solve Multi-Stage Motion Problems

01

Segment by Acceleration Rule

You cannot use one formula across the whole story if acceleration changes! Cut the timeline into stages whenever the object shifts between speeding up, cruising ($a = 0$), or braking.

02

The "Baton Pass" Rule

Velocity cannot jump instantaneously. The final velocity of Stage 1 ($v_{f,1}$) is handed off to become the starting velocity of Stage 2 ($v_{0,2}$). Never assume $v_0 = 0$ for later stages!

03

Local $\Delta t$ vs. Master Clock $t$

Kinematic formulas use duration $\Delta t_n$ within each stage. To get the overall trip time, add stage durations: $t_{\text{total}} = \Delta t_1 + \Delta t_2 + \Delta t_3$.

04

Total Distance from $v-t$ Area

The total displacement is the sum of the areas under each stage of the $v-t$ graph. Triangles give $\frac{1}{2}b h$, rectangles give $b h$. Total $\Delta x = \Delta x_1 + \Delta x_2 + \Delta x_3$.
